### Analytical Overview

On Sunday, January 9, 2000, a lopsided presidential election in Uzbekistan, a fierce Russian military retaliation and border lockdown in Chechnya following the Argun–Shali rebel counteroffensive, and a massive Tel Aviv demonstration against relinquishing the Golan Heights converged with U.S. Pentagon disclosures on the Iraqi no-fly zone and a strong magnitude 6.5 earthquake in the Fiji region. In Uzbekistan, incumbent President Islam Karimov won a further five-year term with 91.9% of the vote in an election dismissed by the Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe (`OSCE`) as lacking genuine competition after his sole nominal opponent, Abdulhafiz Jalolov, told reporters at the ballot box that he had cast his own vote for Karimov. In the North Caucasus, Russian commanders ended their Orthodox Christmas bombing pause, unleashed heavy airstrikes and tactical missile fire against Argun, Shali, and Grozny after acknowledging 26 soldiers killed in Chechen raids, and ordered Chechnya's borders sealed to all males between the ages of 10 and 60.

In the Middle East and the United States, an estimated 150,000 Israelis packed Rabin Square in Tel Aviv under pouring rain to oppose Prime Minister Ehud Barak's proposed Golan Heights withdrawal even as U.S. President Bill Clinton returned to Shepherdstown, West Virginia for late-night mediation with Barak and Syrian Foreign Minister Farouk al-Sharaa. On the same day, the U.S. Department of Defense reported that Iraqi air-defense batteries had fired on coalition aircraft enforcing Operation Southern Watch more than 420 times since December 1998. Meanwhile, a magnitude 6.5 earthquake struck the Fiji region (`usp0009kz2`), boards of directors at America Online (`AOL`) and Time Warner met secretly in New York to approve the largest corporate merger in history, German Catholic bishops debated Pope John Paul II's order to withdraw from Germany's state abortion-counseling system, the 26th People's Choice Awards honored *The Sixth Sense* in Pasadena, and British aerospace pioneer Sir Arnold Hall and Scottish historical novelist Nigel Tranter died.

* **What Happened:**
  * Born in Liverpool on April 23, 1915 and educated at Clare College, Cambridge under Sir Frank Whittle and Sir Melvill Jones, Arnold Alexander Hall designed gyroscopic gunsights and wind tunnels at the Royal Aircraft Establishment (`RAE`) Farnborough during World War II, became Zaharoff Professor of Aviation at Imperial College London at age 30, and was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society (`FRS`) in 1953 at age 38.
  * Appointed Director of the `RAE` in 1951 at age 36, Hall was tasked by Prime Minister Winston Churchill in 1954 with investigating why two *de Havilland Comet 1* jetliners (BOAC Flight 781 off Elba and South African Airways Flight 201 off Naples) had broken apart in mid-air; by submerging an entire Comet fuselage (*G-ALYU*) in a specially built water tank at Farnborough and subjecting it to repeated cabin-pressurization and wing-flexing cycles, Hall proved within weeks that cyclic metal fatigue radiating from stress concentrations at square cabin apertures had ruptured the pressurized fuselage.

* **Why It Matters:**
  * Sir Arnold Hall's 1954 Farnborough Comet investigation laid the foundation of modern aircraft structural-fatigue testing, fail-safe pressurized fuselage design, and scientific air-crash reconstruction worldwide.
