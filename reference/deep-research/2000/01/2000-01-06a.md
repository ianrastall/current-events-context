### Event 6: The last living Pyrenean ibex (Celia) is found crushed by a fallen tree in Spain's Ordesa National Park, rendering the subspecies extinct

* **What Happened:**
  * Decimated by centuries of overhunting and competition with domestic livestock across the Pyrenees, the population of the Pyrenean ibex (*bucardo*, *Capra pyrenaica pyrenaica*) had dwindled by the late 1990s to a single elderly female named Celia in the Ordesa valley of northern Spain.
  * In April 1999, Spanish wildlife veterinarians led by Alberto Fernández-Arias had captured Celia, preserved skin-biopsy cells in liquid nitrogen at laboratories in Zaragoza and Madrid for future somatic-cell cloning attempts, and fitted her with a radio-telemetry collar before releasing her back into Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park.
  * On Thursday, January 6, 2000, rangers tracking a sustained mortality beep from Celia's radio collar found her crushed beneath a fallen beech tree with a fractured skull, formally ending the natural existence of the subspecies.
* **Why It Matters:**
  * Celia's death on January 6, 2000 became one of the milestones of modern conservation biology: using her cryopreserved skin cells acquired nine months earlier, a Franco-Spanish scientific team in 2003 cloned a live female Pyrenean ibex calf that survived for seven minutes before dying of a lung defect, making the Pyrenean ibex the first taxon in history to undergo "de-extinction" and become extinct twice.

January 6, 2000 demonstrated the interplay of high-stakes diplomacy, domestic legal mobilization, and irreversible environmental loss at the close of the first week of the new millennium.
