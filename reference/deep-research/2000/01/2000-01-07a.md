### Analytical Overview

On Friday, January 7, 2000, official Chinese acknowledgment of the 17th Karmapa Lama's flight from Tibet, a massive Muslim protest rally in Jakarta over the Maluku sectarian bloodshed, and the historic swearing-in of Beverley McLachlin as the first female Chief Justice of Canada coincided with escalating legal maneuvers in the Elián González custody battle and a record close on Wall Street. In Beijing, the State Council Information Office and Xinhua confirmed for the first time that 14-year-old Ogyen Trinley Dorje—the 17th Karmapa and the highest Tibetan Buddhist lama recognized by both Beijing and the Dalai Lama—had secretly left Tsurphu Monastery, asserting that he had left behind a letter stating he had gone abroad only to retrieve "black hats and musical instruments" used by previous Karmapas. In Jakarta, more than 100,000 Muslim demonstrators led by People's Consultative Assembly (`MPR`) Speaker Amien Rais rallied at the National Monument (*Monas*) to demand a *jihad* (holy war) in the Maluku islands if President Abdurrahman Wahid failed to halt Christian–Muslim bloodshed within weeks.

In Ottawa, Justice Beverley McLachlin took the oath of office as the 17th Chief Justice of Canada, becoming the first woman to lead the Supreme Court of Canada. In Melbourne, 86-year-old suspected Latvian Nazi collaborator Konrad Kalejs arrived on a flight from London after being ordered deported by British Home Secretary Jack Straw, walking past furious protesters at Tullamarine Airport. Meanwhile, in the United States, Republican Representative Dan Burton issued a congressional subpoena ordering six-year-old Elián González to appear before a House committee in February to block his January 14 return to Cuba, while a strong December jobs report (`315,000` new jobs, `4.1%` unemployment) propelled the Dow Jones Industrial Average up 269 points to a record close of `11,522.56`. Elsewhere, Japanese Prime Minister Keizō Obuchi arrived in Phnom Penh for the first visit to Cambodia by a Japanese premier in 43 years, Turkish security forces stormed a rioting ward at Bandırma Prison, a magnitude 5.5 earthquake struck the Falkland Islands region, and Soviet-Chechen dance legend Makhmud Esambayev, German actor Klaus Wennemann, and American wrestler Gary Albright died.
