## Event: Backlash grows over DOJ criminal investigation of Fed Chair Jerome Powell
* **Category & Subcategory:** Politics and economy / Central bank independence
* **Geography:** United States, Washington, D.C.
* **Summary:** Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell disclosed on the evening of Sunday, January 11 that the Justice Department had served the Fed with grand jury subpoenas on Friday, January 9. The subpoenas threatened a criminal indictment over his June 2025 Senate Banking Committee testimony about the Fed's $2.5 billion headquarters renovation. The story dominated coverage on Monday, January 12. Powell called the action a pretext, saying the threat stemmed from the Fed "setting interest rates based on our best assessment" rather than "the preferences of the president." Trump told NBC News he did not know about the subpoenas. On Monday, Republican Senator Lisa Murkowski called the probe "nothing more than an attempt at coercion," backing Senator Thom Tillis's stance. A joint "Statement on the Federal Reserve" was released the same day, signed by former Fed Chairs Alan Greenspan, Ben Bernanke and Janet Yellen, former Treasury Secretaries Geithner, Paulson, Rubin and Lew, and economists including Glenn Hubbard and Kenneth Rogoff. It said: "This is how monetary policy is made in emerging markets with weak institutions." Markets fell only modestly: S&P 500 futures were down less than 1% in early trading, and Reuters reported the dollar "reels." CNBC/Reuters reported that spot gold hit a record intraday high of $4,629.94 an ounce.
* **What Happened:**
    * Powell said the DOJ served "grand jury subpoenas, threatening a criminal indictment" over his June testimony.
    * Senator Lisa Murkowski, after speaking with Powell on Monday, called the investigation an attempt at coercion.
    * Former Fed Chairs Greenspan, Bernanke and Yellen, former Treasury Secretaries and economists including Hubbard and Rogoff issued a joint "Statement on the Federal Reserve" on Monday. They compared the probe to monetary policy "in emerging markets with weak institutions."
    * A spokesman for Attorney General Pam Bondi declined to comment on the probe, per the WSJ.
    * S&P 500 futures fell less than 1% at the open of pre-market trading; spot gold hit a record $4,629.94 an ounce (CNBC/Reuters).
* **Why It Matters:**
    * A criminal investigation of a sitting Fed chair is without precedent and directly tests the central bank's independence ahead of Powell's chair term ending in May 2026.

## Event: ICJ opens merits hearings in The Gambia v. Myanmar Rohingya genocide case
* **Category & Subcategory:** Law and justice / International courts
* **Geography:** Netherlands, The Hague; Myanmar, Rakhine State; Bangladesh, Cox's Bazar
* **Summary:** On January 12, the International Court of Justice opened three weeks of public hearings on the merits of The Gambia's 2019 case accusing Myanmar of breaching the 1948 Genocide Convention in its treatment of the Rohingya. The case centres on the military's 2017 "clearance operations," which drove at least 730,000 Rohingya into Bangladesh. Gambian Justice Minister Dawda Jallow told judges the Rohingya had "been targeted for destruction." It is the first genocide case the ICJ has heard in full in more than a decade, and Reuters and Al Jazeera said the outcome will likely affect South Africa's case against Israel. Myanmar denies genocide and was represented in court by Union Minister Ko Ko Hlaing. The Gambia presents through January 15, Myanmar responds from January 16, and closed witness sessions will follow; hearings run to January 29.
* **What Happened:**
    * Hearings opened at the Peace Palace under ICJ President Iwasawa Yuji.
    * Jallow told the court: "It is about real people, real stories and a real group of human beings."
    * Myanmar's Ko Ko Hlaing attended; Myanmar maintains its 2017 operations were counterterrorism.
    * The court scheduled closed sessions to hear Rohingya witnesses, the first time Rohingya victims will be heard directly by an international court.
* **Why It Matters:**
    * The ruling will set precedent on how genocide is defined and proven, with implications for South Africa v. Israel.
    * It is a rare accountability venue for crimes committed by Myanmar's military, which has ruled since the 2021 coup.
* **Actors:**
    * Primary: The Gambia (Justice Minister Dawda Jallow); Myanmar (Union Minister Ko Ko Hlaing); International Court of Justice
    * Secondary: Rohingya refugees and victims; UN Independent Investigative Mechanism for Myanmar (Nicholas Koumjian); 11 intervening states

## Event: ISRO's PSLV-C62 fails, losing EOS-N1 and co-passenger satellites
* **Category & Subcategory:** Science and technology / Space launch failure
* **Geography:** India, Sriharikota (Satish Dhawan Space Centre)
* **Summary:** The Indian Space Research Organisation's PSLV-C62, the 64th PSLV flight and ISRO's first launch of 2026, lifted off from Sriharikota at 10:18:30 IST on January 12. It failed to reach its intended orbit after an anomaly in the third stage. The rocket carried the primary payload EOS-N1 ("Anvesha"), a DRDO-developed Earth observation satellite, along with about 14 to 15 co-passenger satellites from Indian and international customers, in a NewSpace India Limited commercial mission. The Space Review reported that the first two stages performed nominally. This was the PSLV's second consecutive failure after PSLV-C61 in 2025.
* **What Happened:**
